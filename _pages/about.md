I am a Scientist II at the [Allen Institute](https://alleninstitute.org/), where I work in the Brain Health Accelerator. I develop machine learning methods for personalized biology and health, with a focus on Alzheimer's disease and other neurodegenerative conditions.

My work centers on generative and multimodal models for large-scale single-cell and spatial data. I use representation learning, attention, and graph-based modeling to connect cellular states and tissue context with individual disease outcomes. I enjoy working hands-on across the full pipeline, from data harmonization and model implementation through training, evaluation, and biological interpretation.

Current work
======

- **scVIP:** a hierarchical generative model with cell-type-aware attention that links single-cell expression and composition to individual phenotypes. It predicts held-out cortical developmental age (Pearson r = 0.95) and Huntington's disease burden (concordance correlation = 0.90), and identifies disease-associated cell populations across neurodegenerative and immune cohorts.
- **spaVI:** a generative graph neural network that links shared spatial programs to cell-type-specific expression changes using heterotypic neighbor attention. Integrating Xenium spatial data with matched snRNA-seq in Alzheimer's disease caudate, it recovers eight spatial dimensions, compared with four from Xenium alone.
- **Multiomic foundation models:** extending a transformer-based single-cell RNA model toward paired scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq representation learning, and exploring diffusion-based methods to combine regulatory and transcriptional signals.
- **SEA-AD DREAM Challenge:** I co-organized this community challenge benchmarking computational methods, including AI agents, for predicting Alzheimer's disease neuropathology from multi-regional single-cell data.

Background
======

Before joining the Allen Institute, I was a postdoctoral fellow in Statistics at Harvard University with Professor Susan Murphy. In the Statistical Reinforcement Learning Lab, I developed reinforcement learning methods for personalized mobile health interventions and built tools that let behavioral scientists design and run Thompson sampling–based studies.

I received my M.S. and Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering and Computer Science from MIT, advised by Professors Thomas Heldt and Vivienne Sze (Ph.D.) and Professor Alan Oppenheim (M.S.). My doctoral research used smartphone cameras and computer vision to measure eye movements, and Bayesian models to track individual neurocognitive state over time, for remote monitoring of neurodegenerative disease. Before that, I earned a B.S. in Electrical Engineering and Mathematics from National Taiwan University.
